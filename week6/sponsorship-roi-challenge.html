Sponsorship return on investment challenge

The Biscayne Bay Sports Group earns millions from sponsors, and renewal season is here. Calculate what each deal delivers, decide which sponsors to renew, renegotiate, or replace, and match sponsors to activation ideas that turn fans into customers.

How to use this challenge
  1. Part 1 (36 points): calculate cost per thousand impressions, cost per lead, and return on investment for four sponsors.
  2. Part 2 (36 points): use the numbers to decide what to do with six sponsors at renewal time.
  3. Part 3 (28 points): match four sponsors to the activation idea that best fits their goal.
  4. Each part is checked when you press its button. Your final score appears at the end.

The figures come from the course file sponsors.csv. "Attributed sales" are the sponsor's own sales that they can trace back to the partnership, for example through coupon codes.

Part 1: run the numbers

Cost per thousand impressions (CPM)annual fee ÷ media impressions × 1,000. What it costs to put the brand in front of 1,000 people. Lower is cheaper reach.
Cost per leadannual fee ÷ leads generated. What it costs to collect one interested customer (an email, a test drive, a sign-up). Lower is better.
Return on investment (ROI)(attributed sales − annual fee) ÷ annual fee × 100. Above 0% the sponsor earned back more than it paid.

Round CPM and cost per lead to cents and ROI to one decimal place. Answers within 2% (or within 1 point for ROI) count as correct.

Part 2: renewal decisions

For each sponsor choose one action. Renew keeps the deal as it is. Renegotiate changes the price or adds activations so it works better. Replace means preparing a new partner for the category.

Part 3: match the activation

An activation is something a sponsor does with fans, beyond showing a logo. The best ones fit the sponsor's product and can be measured.
