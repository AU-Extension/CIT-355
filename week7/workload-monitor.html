How to use this interactive
  1. Pick a team (soccer or basketball), then pick a player from the list or click a row in the squad table.
  2. Read the top chart: bars are each week's training load, the dark line is the four-week average, and a red cross marks a week with an injury.
  3. Read the bottom chart: the workload ratio compares this week with the four-week average. Green is a typical week, amber and red mean a sudden jump.
  4. Move the slider to plan week 13 and watch the ratio and the warning zone update.
  5. Answer the four quick questions at the bottom to check your understanding.

What is the workload ratio?

Training load is a single number for how hard a player worked in a week (here: minutes of training multiplied by how hard each session felt, on a 1 to 10 scale). The workload ratio divides this week's load by the average of the last four weeks, including this week.

A ratio near 1.0 means "about the same as usual." A ratio of 1.5 means this week was much heavier than the player is used to. Sports scientists treat sudden jumps as a warning sign for injury risk. In this tool the zones are: below 0.8 underloaded, 0.8 to 1.3 typical, 1.3 to 1.5 caution, above 1.5 high.
